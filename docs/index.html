HEARsay Mysteries

Engaging mystery games that aim to help teach the concept of conversational receptiveness.

Each game is a mystery on a university campus. You interview student witnesses who hold different views on a current campus controversy, and you have to solve the mystery before your questions run out. You get the most out of the witnesses by using the HEAR framework, which comes from Julia Minson's research on conversational receptiveness:

  • Hedge your claims
  • Emphasize agreement
  • Acknowledge the other perspective
  • Reframe to the positive

A game takes about 45 to 75 minutes. Progress can be saved from the sidebar (Saves); saves are kept in the browser you're playing in.

Mysteries

  • The Commons' Comma

    The night before the university publicly examines its founding Charter to settle whether a disputed ink mark is a comma, the Charter vanishes from the archive. You have until five o'clock to find out who took it, why, and where it is, by interviewing eight students who each know something and each have an opinion about the controversial renovation of the commons - which the comma/ink blot would decide.

    Play

Game design: Tomer Perry. The HEAR Framework is based on the work of Julia Minson and the Constructive Disagreement Lab at the Harvard Kennedy School. All characters and events are fictional.
